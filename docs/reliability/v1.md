## 2. The soak

`scripts/soak/`:

| File | Does |
|---|---|
| `soak.ps1` | Writes a config with 5 SFTP profiles (`keyonly` with the test key, *Mount on start*, letters from Z down) and the server's host key into a scratch home under `target\soak\<time>`, starts the release `drivemount.exe` with `APPDATA`, `LOCALAPPDATA` and `USERPROFILE` there, and runs the rest. Logs the app's working set, private bytes, handles and threads every minute, flags a call under way for 5 minutes as STUCK, hashes the server's files every hour. Keeps the PC awake between its own sleeps. At the end: stops the load and the drops, checks that each drive takes a write within 5 minutes, hashes the server's files, kills the app and checks that no letter is left |
| `soak-worker.ps1` | One per drive: write a file from a local pool (0 B to 16 MiB, NFR-REL-01's sizes among them), hash it back, rename it to `<sha256>-<cycle>.bin`, list the folder, reread an older file, delete all but the last 10 |
| `soak-net.sh` | In WSL as root: every 20 minutes, `iptables -j DROP` on the container's address in `DOCKER-USER`, for 120 s |
| `soak-report.ps1` | `report.md`: PASS only with no mismatch on the drive or the server, no file missing from a listing, no STUCK, no Error state or panic in the app's log, and working-set growth under 10% (median of minutes 30–60 against the last 30). Also a line fitted to private bytes after the warm-up, in MiB an hour, and the errors that fell outside a drop or a sleep |

How to run it (about 4 h 10 min), with nothing else running on the PC and the owner's DriveMount closed:

`-Hours`, `-DropEveryMinutes`, `-DropSeconds`, `-SleepEveryMinutes`, `-SleepSeconds` change the schedule; `-NoSleep` leaves sleep out. The sleeps need *Allow wake timers* on in the power plan (it is, on this PC, and the script checks). The app's window is open throughout; leave it be.

### 2.1 The 4 h run (2026-11-13, 14:52 to 18:52 UTC)

Defaults: 5 drives, 11 drops of 120 s every 20 minutes, 6 sleeps (S3) of 120 s every 40 minutes, about 411,000 cycles in all. Results in `target\soak\run-4h\` (`report.md`).

- **Reconnecting works.** 126 times *Reconnecting*, 126 reconnects, none given up, no panic, no STUCK. Every sleep came back by its timer (124 to 126 s); every drive was *Mounted* again within the backoff after each drop and each wake. At the end all 5 took a write at once, and killing the app left no letter.
- **The server's files:** 4 checks (3 hourly and the final one) of the 40 to 50 files kept at the time, 173 hashes in all, each matching its name.
- **Data: FAIL, 3 events, all at a sleep.**
  - **R:, 18:03:15, MISMATCH.** A 16 MiB `Copy-Item` was under way when the PC went to sleep at 18:01:13. It returned success 120 s later, and the file read back different. The app's log: `writing failed after the program was told it worked status=0xC000023C path=/soak-5/64887.part`.
  - **S:, 17:19:08, and V:, 17:21:12, INVISIBLE.** `Remove-Item` succeeded either side of the 17:19 sleep, but the files stayed. The log: `cleanup failed` for each (`STATUS_IO_TIMEOUT`, `STATUS_NETWORK_UNREACHABLE`).
  - The log has 5 more such failures, at drops, where the worker's next step failed for the drop anyway, so nothing checked the file.
- **Memory: FAIL on the limit, a leak not shown.** Working set 75.6 MiB (median of minutes 30 to 60) against 83.7 MiB (last 30), +10.8%. Private bytes swing between 62 and 102 MiB and fall back after peaks (17:18, 18:23); a fitted line climbs 7.3 MiB an hour. Handles stay at about 385, threads at 51–57. 4 h cannot tell a slow leak from the allocator holding what buffers used.
- **One unexplained error** (V:, 16:14:38.9) is the harness's: the step began 0.1 s before the drop that `net.log` records at whole seconds.

### 2.2 Why: failures at `Cleanup` reach nobody

A write still on its way after `WriteFile` returned (write-behind, §5.6), and the removal of a file deleted on close, both run at WinFsp's `Cleanup`, which cannot return an error: Windows has closed the handle already. ADR-0002 accepted this for writes, as "an error comes one call late". A program that calls `Write` or `Flush` again hears it; one that closes next does not, and `CopyFile` and `DeleteFile` close next.

The soak shows it is not rare. The work at `Cleanup` waits at most `op_timeout_secs` (15 s), while a drop or a sleep lasts minutes. After a sleep it is worse: the deadline has passed on waking, so the call fails at once, before the reconnect has had a chance. `SessionFs` would repeat a write at an offset on the next session, but the deadline above it ends the wait first.

Ways out, as put to the owner (the owner chose 2):

1. **`Cleanup` waits for the next session** for its writes and its delete, beyond `op_timeout_secs`, with a cap (e.g. the 2 minutes a sleep or drop took here). Simple; `CloseHandle` hangs while the server is away, and Explorer with it.
2. **Keep the work and finish it after the reconnect.** `CloseHandle` returns; up to 4 MiB a handle stays in memory and is sent, or the file removed, once a session is back; listings and opens of that path see the pending state. Nothing hangs, but more to build, and lost if the app ends first.
3. **Delete in `SetDelete`,** where an error can still be returned, rather than at `Cleanup`. Fixes the deletes only.
4. **Accept it** and say so in the spec, as Windows' own "Delayed Write Failed" does.

### 2.3 The fix: owed work finishes after the reconnect (2026-11-13)

The owner chose way 2. Built in `crates/drivemount-winfsp` (`owed.rs`, the adapter's write-behind, `Cleanup` and `Close`); spec §5.6 and [ADR-0002](../adr/0002-write-behind.md)'s amendment describe it:

- A background write that the connection fails (timeout or lost connection) waits for the next session and goes again, keeping its data and its room. A `Flush` meanwhile fails, so a program that asks hears that the server is away.
- `Cleanup` without a session does not wait. A closed handle with writes owed stays among the open files (reads wait for it, renames reach it) until they arrive.
- A removal at `Cleanup` that the connection fails goes again once it is back; "not found" then counts. Lookups and listings wait for it.
- Stopping the drive ends what still waits, with a Warn line.

**Tests** (`write_path.rs`, on `MemFs`; 5 of them fail without the fix): `writes_on_their_way_when_the_connection_goes_arrive_once_it_is_back`, `a_flush_while_the_connection_is_away_fails_and_the_writes_still_arrive`, `writes_owed_after_a_close_follow_a_rename`, `a_file_deleted_while_the_connection_is_away_goes_once_it_is_back`, `a_file_already_gone_once_the_connection_is_back_counts_as_removed`, `a_removal_that_timed_out_goes_again`, `unmounting_ends_what_waits_for_the_connection`. `a_hung_write_times_out_and_the_next_can_work` became `a_hung_write_times_out_for_the_flush_and_goes_again`: a write that timed out is no longer dropped. The whole workspace passes, and `drivemount-winfsp` also over SFTP; clippy is clean.

**A 20-minute soak** (`-Hours 0.34 -DropEveryMinutes 5 -DropSeconds 60 -SleepEveryMinutes 7 -SleepSeconds 60`, `target\soak
un-fix1\`): about 28,600 cycles, 3 drops, 2 sleeps, 35 reconnects, none given up. No mismatch, nothing invisible, no unexplained error; the 50 files on the server all match. The new path ran twice: after the 20:05 sleep a copy's last writes arrived 5 s after waking (that file, an interrupted cycle's `.part`, was then removed as asked), and after the 20:11 drop another's arrived on the reconnect and its hash checked. The report's FAIL is memory alone, measured from minute 2 as in §2.4; it says nothing over 20 minutes.

### 2.4 A 15-minute run of the scripts (2026-11-13)

`-Hours 0.25 -DropEveryMinutes 4 -DropSeconds 60 -NoSleep`: 4 drops, about 24,000 cycles over the 5 drives.

- **No mismatch,** on the drives or among the 50 files hashed on the server at the end. No file missing from a listing.
- **Errors only during drops,** 4 to 7 a drive, each after the 15 s operation timeout (NFR-REL-03). The longest step was a 16 MiB `Copy-Item` of 71 s: several callbacks, each within its timeout.
- **Each drop:** the connections end about 19 s in, every drive goes to *Reconnecting*, and is *Mounted* again about 11 s after the network is back (the backoff). At the end all 5 took a write at once, and killing the app left no letter.
- **Memory:** private bytes 15 MiB at start, then between 33 and 78 MiB, rising under load and falling after each reconnect; handles flat at about 385, threads 52–57. 15 minutes cannot tell a leak from buffers being filled (write-behind holds up to 4 MiB a handle, and the pool's largest file is 16 MiB), so the report's FAIL on growth (48%, measured from minute 2) means nothing here; the 4 h run measures from minute 30.

**What the drop looks like:** the client does not see a silent network. Windows' connection to WSL is reset after about 19 s (`os error 10054`), so the app sees a closed connection rather than keep-alives going unanswered. Both lead to the same *Reconnecting*.

### 2.5 Found while writing the scripts

- **PowerShell 5.1 puts a BOM before piped text,** so `sh` in the container read `﻿cat`. Scripts now go as base64 on the command line.
- **The heartbeat files raced,** a read finding a file being rewritten. With `$ErrorActionPreference = 'Stop'` that ended the run, and its worker jobs with it, silently (the app and the dropper went on). Reads and writes of those files now skip a round instead, the watch loop logs a fault of its own as `harness-error` and goes on, and `console.log` keeps the console.
